5 steps to take right away

  1. Stop all contact

    Don't reply, click, or transfer anything more. Hang up the call. Scammers often come back with "recovery" offers, which are also scams.

  2. Verify through an official channel

    Not sure if it's real? Contact the organisation using the number on its official website or your card, never the one in the message.

  3. Call your bank immediately

    Ask them to freeze your account and cards, and stop or recall any transfers. Change your banking passwords. If you installed an unknown app, switch your phone to flight mode first.

  4. Report to the police

    File a report online or at a police station. Keep evidence: screenshots, phone numbers, bank account numbers and transaction references. Don't delete the chats.

  5. Warn others

    Tell family and friends, and report the number or account on the platform and on ScamShield. Your report could stop the next victim.

Protect yourself before it happens

  1. Install the ScamShield app

    It filters scam SMS and blocks known scam calls.

  2. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)

    On your email, social media, banking and gaming accounts. Never share the code with anyone.

  3. Lower your transaction limits

    Set daily PayNow and transfer limits in your banking app so losses stay small if something goes wrong. Turn on transaction alerts.

  4. Use your bank's money lock

    Many banks let you lock savings so they can't be moved digitally.

  5. Agree on a family safe word

    If a "family member" calls in a panic asking for money, ask for the word. This defeats voice-clone scams.

  6. Talk about it

    Share what you learn with parents and grandparents. Scams thrive on silence and shame.
